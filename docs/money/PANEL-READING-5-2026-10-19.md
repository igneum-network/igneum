## 1. The bound, from the rule (the review lead, the security review)

Read from section 2 of the page against the code (ghostdag.go credit(), less(); dagform.go dagWork, dagLookup), not from the run.

- The unit (i): dagWork returns 2^32 for any header with a coordinate; verifyVectorHeader makes a header's difficulty its lane's controller output at its parent, so D / D_in_force is one for every valid block. Correct, and it restores the design's third only in the steady state; the page says so.
- The lemma: the induction over ordered positions has three transitions (a credit of L, a credit of another lane, no credit) and the counts move only by credits carried from the selected parent; credit() implements exactly that (cr copied from spd.Credits, raised only on a credit, stored as nd.Credits). The lemma holds for the lanes in the minimum at the crediting block; the code's test is `cr[lane] >= least+slack` against the least over the OTHER lanes in the minimum, so the credited lane's own membership is never read (F-811 below).
- Theorem 1: cr_c >= cr_L - S for every other c, so T >= n cr_L - (n - 1) S and cr_L / T <= 1/n + (n - 1) S / (n T). At n = 3, S = 4: 1/3 + 8 / (3 T); 33.84 percent at T = 524 (the 10x lane's 174 / 524 = 33.21 is inside); under 1/3 + 0.01 from T >= 267 (8 / (3 T) < 0.01 at T > 266.7). Correct.
- Theorem 2: the two lemma instances subtract to D_L <= D_c + 2 S, so over a window D_L / T_w <= 1/3 + 4 S / (3 T_w); 3.6 percent at T_w = 150. Correct. The bound reaches one at T_w = 2 S = 8, so a window of 8 or fewer credited units bounds nothing; the page wrote "8 S" in two places (F-808, re-cut to 2 S = 8).
- Theorem 3: a branch that credits no block of lane j has cr_L(B) <= cr_j(A) + S for every present L and cr_L(A) >= cr_j(A) - S, so it gains at most 2 S a lane, 2 (n - 1) S = 16 in all; the least-credited lane on a branch with every lane sealed is always under every other's count plus S, so always credited: the honest chain gains at least one unit a least-lane block. Correct, and the 17-block figure follows (3.4 minutes at the 12-second lane target). What the theorem does not cover, the page states in (a) to (c): the merging branch, the tie on the second key, the floor regime. the review's reading of (a) is F-809.
- The second key: dag.less orders by blue work, then blue score, then the hash; dagPreferByWeight and TemplateParents read dag.Less; a Lookup without Lanes credits every blue, so the Kaspa vectors and a chain without the vector form keep the papers' order (TestGhostdagAgainstKaspaVectors and TestFormBWithoutLanesIsThePapersWork pass on the merged tree; question 5 of the fourth reading answered).

## 2. The silent lane, first (the security review's hardest question; F-810)

The question the fourth reading put first: a lane of the minimum stops; what does the network do for payments, is the chain live or stalled, what do the two-lane figures mean for a user.

- The chain is LIVE, not stalled. On 7412 lane 2's sealers stopped at 11:32:07Z; through 73 minutes the other two lanes sealed 609 chain blocks, 200 of the record's 300 transactions were included, ordered, executed and paid, the ordering hash equal on all eight nodes at 998 of 998 orders, no red outside the peering merge, one tip everywhere. Payments go through. What a user sees is the rate: with two lanes the merged interval is 6 seconds against 4, so CONFIRMED at depth 3 read 22.9 s at the median and 44.0 s at p95 against 14.1 / 26.8 before the stop; included 4.3 s against 2.8. The two-lane figures are the rate's cost and nothing else; a wallet reading depth 3 waits half as long again.
- What stops is the WEIGHT. By the rule the other lanes credit four more each (the stopped lane's count plus S) and then nothing: the credits froze at 106 / 106 / 102 from chain block 477 to the restart, as the page predicted from the lemma. From then until the lane returned the first key was constant on every tip, so the head was chosen by the second key, the blue score, Kaspa's additive count: form A at unit work. For the outage the chip resistance at consensus is suspended in exactly the way step 0 found it: by rate each lane's controller still holds its lane at one block in 12 seconds, so a chip on a remaining lane holds no more of the count than its lane's share; by transient (a lane returning at a decayed difficulty, a chip starting cold before its controller lifts it) the count follows the hash rate for a half-life. 7412 read the freeze with one tip and no race, so nothing was decided by the count; the panel does not read that as a pass of the degraded state, and neither does the page.
- the security review's reading: a lane can be SILENCED as well as lost. A party holding a chip on lane A that stops lane C's few sealers (the thinnest lane of a young network has one or two) puts the chain into the frozen state at will, and in that state its chip's transient is the fork choice. So the frozen state is an attack surface, not only an outage cost, and the hypothesis of the design's section 5 ("at any rate, at any cost") must count the silencing among the costs. Under VECTORWORK's chain form the served band takes a decayed lane out of the minimum (an hour and a half at the lab's constants), which a merging per-block credit cannot read at a common ancestor (the review lead's b9ff75f28 ruling has no object in a DAG): the page's section 7 states the choice honestly and leaves it to the review.
- The panel's answer (F-810, written onto the page): the frozen state stands as the RECORDED cost of this step, the record being a measurement; for the next design revision the panel's default is the band from the controller with a finality depth under the floor's decay time (Kaspa's finality rule: a tip whose selected chain lacks the node's finality point is refused, so a branch that waited out a lane's decay in private cannot be released). The default carries its own row (a private branch that lets a lane decay, released at the finality depth plus one) and its own cost (a partition longer than the finality depth splits the network until a hand joins it), both for the design page, not for this landing. The silencing row (the thinnest lane's sealers stopped, a chip on another lane cold-started) is written into the attacks list beside the withholding row.
- The one-line answer, for main: live and paying through the outage at the two-lane rate (depth 3 in 22.9 s against 14.1), the weight frozen so the head is Kaspa's count for the outage and the chip resistance at consensus is suspended for a lane's transient; silencing a lane is therefore an attack, and the panel's default cure for the next design cut is the band with a finality depth.

## 3. The four other questions

**The controllers' noise (a fifth of honest blocks uncredited at S = 4): a cost to miners' pay?** No. Every blue block is paid at its order by the block that merges it, credited or not (finalizeDAG, unchanged from step 0; the page's section 7 says it and 7413's lane 2 was paid 360 blocks for a third of the weight); the lane controllers count merged blues, not credits; reds are the k-cluster rule's and the peering merges' alone. The cost is to the honest chain's WEIGHT: it grows at about 0.77 of its block count on 7412 (305 credits of 398 blues at the stop) and 0.68 on 7413 (524 of 775), which lengthens nothing of theorem 3 (the 17 blocks are least-lane blocks, always credited) and shortens the honest margin over a bounded attacker in proportion. the review's number: S stays k = 4 for this record (one anticone of one lane's blocks; every bound of section 2 scales with S); the clipping is the absolute controllers' lead variance (near six blocks at the 600-second half-life) against a slack of four, and the design item is the controllers' lead, not a larger S. The driver should report the honest credit fraction beside the share (the page's own next-cut item for T_w, extended).

**The merging attacker's margin (F-809).** Section 2 (a) bounds a branch that merges the public DAG at 2 (n - 1) S = 16 units over the public tip, by the same subtraction as theorem 3 with the public lane's credits inherited. At unit work 16 units is 16 ORDERS, and the ladder's CONFIRMED depth is 3. So on paper a party holding the majority of two lanes' hash rate (the party the minimum rule was written against) can hold a private branch over the public DAG, merging the honest third lane's blocks, and release it to reorder a depth-3 confirmation; its own blocks credit while they stay within S of the merged lane's count, so the margin persists at every release. the security review's second reading tightens it: a private branch of more than k = 4 blocks puts more than k blue blocks into the anticone of every public block sealed after them, the honest third lane's included, so those go red on the released branch and the branch's credit freezes at the fork's third-lane count plus S a lane; the honest chain then outgrows it at one unit a least-lane block (theorem 3's clock, 3.4 minutes). A branch that keeps the public third lane's blocks blue holds at most k private blocks, so its margin is at most k units plus the honest lanes' clipping lag. The depth that survives a release therefore lies between k = 4 and 2 (n - 1) S = 16 orders plus the lag (16 to 64 seconds at the lab's merged rate), above the ladder's 3 either way; and a released branch reds the honest blocks of the attacker's two lanes sealed during the withholding, which are then unpaid. The page's "no confirmation reversed at any depth" is a figure of the honest three-lane rows and is now marked so; the withholding row measures the depth, and the ladder's confirmation target is set from that row, not from the honest rows. Faster blocks (the next rung) shorten the seconds, not the orders: k and S rise with 2 D lambda.

**A chip holding one lane: what it gains under this rule.** Its lane's pay in full (7413's lane 2: 360 of 775 blue blocks, 46.5 percent, every one ordered and paid; the other sealers of that lane earn nothing, which the design's pay row accepts), a third of the weight and no more (174 of 524 credited, 33.2 percent, against the bound's 33.8; the step-0 form would have given it about 70 percent of the added blue work at its lifted difficulty), and on the ordering the same bounded margin as any one-lane withholder (2 S = 8 units at most; k when it keeps the others' blocks blue). The confirmation figures were better than step 0's with the chip lane sealing (12.1 / 24.9 against 14.4 / 30.9 at depth 3): the extra blocks before its controller settled, not the rule.

**The lab fault's effect on the row.** The lane-stopped-and-restarted row was briefed as one row and ran as two halves 73 minutes apart because the row script's miner.start(2) was refused (this node's miner_start takes no count; miner.start() is every core; no setThreads) and nothing read the refusal. The stop half is the briefed row and reads as the rule predicts (the freeze at the stopped count plus S). The restart half is a STRONGER row than briefed: the lane came back at a difficulty decayed to 0.014 of its reference and sealed about a block a second across its two sealers for a quarter of an hour, and the credit held it within four of the least lane throughout (106 / 106 / 110 to 188 / 185 / 189 together). The briefed restart (a lane back after ten minutes at a difficulty near its reference) was not run; its figures would lie between the stop half's and step 0's and nothing on the page claims them. The record's two-lane confirmation figures (n = 200) are the 73-minute outage's. The committed script was left wrong on purpose "as the record of the fault"; the panel does not land a script known wrong on main (F-812: lanestart is now the sealer's process restarted from its own start.sh at its configured thread count, the enodes re-applied when given, every answer read and an error stopping the row; the fault's record is the page's section 6 item 1).

## 4. The chip-resistance-at-consensus hypothesis: its status

The design's section 5 states it as a hypothesis to test and not a property: "a party that can produce one or two lanes' blocks at any rate, at any cost, holds no authority over the ordering and earns on the pay rule no more than its weakest lane's standing". After this step:

- MEASURED AT ONE POINT for the weight: one lane at ten times the others' hash rate from the genesis, 33.2 percent of the credited work against the bound's 33.8, on one chain, one run, three lanes, the null algorithm, two sealers a lane, the lab's latency. The bound behind the point is proved from the rule (section 1), so the point is not a coincidence of the run; it is still one point.
- NOT MEASURED for the ordering authority: the withholding row, the balance row, the two-lane row and the latency rows are unrun, and the review's reading of the rule (F-809) puts a two-lane majority's reach on paper between k and 2 (n - 1) S orders at every release, above the ladder's CONFIRMED depth. The hypothesis's "no authority" is false on paper at depth 3 and bounded at the merge depth; what it is at the depth between is the withholding row's number.
- SUSPENDED during a lane outage (section 2): the frozen state hands the head to the count for the outage, and a lane can be silenced.
- The pay half ("no more than its weakest lane's standing") is unchanged by this step and unmeasured by it: a one-lane chip earns its whole lane, as the design's pay row accepts.

So: a hypothesis with one measured point in its favour on the weight, a proof behind that point, and a paper reading against it on the ordering at the ladder's confirmation depth. Not a property. The ADR's "Money remains research" is untouched by this record (the lane DAG is not in the programme).
