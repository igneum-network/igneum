## 2. The rule, and the bound proved from it

Step 0 found that form A as the design writes it (a block's work D(b) / D_ref with the lane's REGISTERED reference) gives a lane the weight of its hash rate: lane 2 back at 48 times its rate had its difficulty lifted to 17.75 times its reference by its controller and each of its blocks then weighed twenty of the others', 87 percent of every block's added blue work. The design's section 2.2 item 2 reasoned the opposite from the controller holding the lane's block RATE; the rate was held, the weight was not, because the reference does not follow the difficulty. This cut changes the measure in two places and leaves the colouring, the selected-parent rule and the mergeset order as Kaspa states them.

**(i) The unit.** A block's own work under the vector form is its difficulty over its lane's difficulty IN FORCE at the block, times 2^32. The difficulty rule (verifyVectorHeader: a header's difficulty is its lane's controller output at its parent, exactly) makes the quotient one for every valid block, so every block of every lane weighs ONE UNIT whatever its lane's hash rate: dagform.go dagWork returns the unit for a tagged header and the chain form's D(b) for a header of no lane (the genesis). This alone restores the design's third in the steady state: the controller holds each lane at one block every 12 seconds, each block weighs one, each lane holds a third. It does not bound the transient (a lane's blocks before its controller has caught up: step 0's 60 of 100 chain blocks in the ten minutes after the restart; this lab's 67 of 60 after its own) and it does nothing against a two-lane specialist, which is why the second change is the one the step is named for.

**(ii) The credit: form B carried as a per-order credit.** The design's form B chooses the selected parent by the MINIMUM over the lanes first, then the additive sum, and the page says why that is a departure from the papers: the minimum is not additive, and GHOSTDAG's colouring and mergeset walk need one additive scalar that grows along the selected chain. This cut keeps the scalar and puts the minimum inside it. Every block x carries, in its ordering data (dag.Data.Credits), a CREDITED COUNT per lane over its blue past. When x is coloured, its mergeset blues are walked in their colouring order (the selected parent first, as Kaspa's form has it) with the counts starting from x's selected parent's; a blue block b of lane L is CREDITED, its unit added to x's blue work and the lane's count raised by one, if and only if

    Credits[L] < min over the other lanes c in the minimum of Credits[c] + S,

else it is blue, ordered and paid exactly as before but adds nothing to the weight. The lanes "in the minimum" at x are the lanes the registration makes sealable at x's height (dagLookup.InMinimum): nothing a sealer does moves a lane in or out (section 7 says why the chain form's served band is not read here). A block of no lane (the genesis) is credited; with no other lane in the minimum a block is credited (nothing to balance against). The slack S is DAGCreditSlack = k = 4, one anticone's worth of one lane's blocks. The blue work of x is its selected parent's plus the units of the blues credited at x; the order among blocks is (blue work, blue score, hash): the credited work first, then the additive count (form B's "then the additive sum", at unit work the blue score itself), then Kaspa's hash tie. The selected parent, the mergeset's walk order, the head (dagPreferByWeight, dag.Less) and the template's parent order all read that one order. A chain without the vector form credits every blue, so the measure there is the papers' blue work exactly and the six rusty-kaspa test DAGs colour as before (TestGhostdagAgainstKaspaVectors unchanged; TestFormBWithoutLanesIsThePapersWork).

This is the variant the brief calls bounded-share. The design page names only the lexicographic form B and leaves its argument to this step, and the reason this cut takes the credit rather than the lexicographic selected-parent rule is stated plainly: a pairwise, branch-relative minimum (the design's "above the common ancestor") is not a total order over blocks, and GHOSTDAG's mergeset walk, its anticone counting and its blue work all need one. The credit gives the minimum's property as an invariant of one additive scalar, provable in four lines and checked by every node at every block; what it gives up against the lexicographic form is section 2's (a) and (b) below.

**The bound, from the rule.** Write n for the number of lanes in the minimum (three on the lab chains) and cr_c(x) for lane c's credited count in x's blue past.

Lemma (the invariant). For every block x and every lane L in the minimum, cr_L(x) <= min over the other lanes c of cr_c(x) + S. Proof by induction over the ordered positions. At the genesis every count is zero. At a position where a block of lane L is credited, the rule required cr_L < min_others + S before the credit, so cr_L <= min_others + S after it. At a position where a block of another lane is credited, min_others for L can only rise or stay, so L's inequality stands; the credited lane's own inequality is the first case. At an uncredited position nothing moves. The counts are carried from the selected parent and raised only by credits, so no other transition exists.

Theorem 1 (the share at any block). For every lane L: cr_L(x) <= (T(x) + (n - 1) S) / n, where T(x) is the sum of the counts; with n = 3, a lane's share of the credited work is at most 1/3 + 2 S / (3 T), under one third plus the design's delta of 0.01 once T >= 267, and 1/3 + 0.5 percent at 7413's 524 units. Proof: by the lemma applied to L, every other lane c has cr_c >= cr_L - S (because cr_L <= min_others + S <= cr_c + S); so T = cr_L + the sum over the others >= cr_L + (n - 1)(cr_L - S) = n cr_L - (n - 1) S.

Theorem 2 (any window). For two blocks p and q on one selected chain, p in q's past, and D_c = cr_c(q) - cr_c(p): for every pair of lanes L and c, D_L <= D_c + 2 S. Proof: cr_L(q) <= cr_c(q) + S (the lemma at q, for L) and cr_c(p) <= cr_L(p) + S (the lemma at p, for c); subtracting the second from the first gives cr_L(q) - cr_L(p) <= cr_c(q) - cr_c(p) + 2 S. Hence over any window whose credited work is T_w, a lane's share is at most 1/3 + 4 S / (3 T_w): 1/3 + 3.6 percent over a 150-order window with every lane crediting (the merge-depth window, the longest any merge can reorder), vacuous under T_w = 2 S = 8 credited units (where 1/3 + 4 S / (3 T_w) reaches one; the first cut wrote 8 S, F-808), and theorem 1's figure over a window that starts at the landing check.

Theorem 3 (a branch without one lane). Let A be a block and B a block above it whose path from A (the selected chain from A to B and the mergesets along it) credits no block of lane j, j in the minimum: a private branch sealed by a party holding the other n - 1 lanes at ANY hash rate, at ANY cost, that does not merge the public DAG. Then every present lane L gains at most 2 S credits from A to B (cr_L(B) <= cr_j(B) + S = cr_j(A) + S, and cr_L(A) >= cr_j(A) - S), the branch gains at most 2 (n - 1) S = 16 units in all, however many blocks it holds; and a branch on which every lane of the minimum is sealed gains at least one unit for every block of its least-credited lane (that lane's count is at or under every other's, so the rule always credits it). So the honest chain outweighs any such branch once its least lane has sealed 17 blocks past A: 3.4 minutes at the lab's 12-second lane target, inside the 10-minute merge-depth window, and independent of the attacker's rate. The two-lane specialist, the case the minimum rule was written for and the row the design says decides between the forms, is bounded on paper by this theorem; its row is still to be RUN (section 8). TestFormBAbsentLaneBranchGainsAtMostTwoSlack reads it on a DAG: a two-lane private branch of 600 blocks gains 8 credits after the fork tip's own, the honest branch of 18 blocks 17, the honest head preferred.

What the theorems do not say, stated here and in section 7: (a) a branch that MERGES the public DAG inherits the public credits, so its margin over the public tip is at most 2 (n - 1) S by the same argument, a constant and not zero: the withholding row of the design's section 5 measures what a constant margin buys at the release against the k-cluster rule and the merge-depth bound; (b) when the credited work ties, the blue score decides, which is form A at unit work: a lane of the minimum that STOPS freezes every other lane's credit at its count plus S after S blocks, and from then until it returns the chain is ordered by the count, the state VECTORWORK.md section 8 (i) and (ii) names for the minimum rule, bounded by the outage's length, and 7412's 73 minutes read it (section 5); (c) nothing here reads a lane's difficulty, so the floor regime of VECTORWORK revision 15 (a) is not a term of this rule, and a lane at the floor stays in the minimum.

## 6. What broke and what was learned

1. **miner_start takes no thread count on this node; the row script's miner.start(2) was refused and the lane stayed out for 73 minutes.** Step 0's lesson ("the thread count named") was written into the script as miner.start(2); the node's console answered "Invalid number of input parameters to RPC method" and the row's output went to row.log as null, which nothing read until the driver's record showed no lane-2 blues after chain block 400. The miner API on this build is start, stop, setEtherbase, setExtra, setGasPrice, setGasLimit, setRecommitInterval, getHashrate: no setThreads, and miner.start() sets every core ("Updated mining threads threads=96"), whatever --miner.threads said at the start. The form that restarts a lane at its count is the sealer's PROCESS restarted from its data directory with its flags (what the hand did at 12:45:50Z), and a row script must read its own command's answer and stop on an error. The committed lanestart is left as the record of the fault; the next lab replaces it with the process restart. The cost: the lane-stopped row's two halves are separated by 73 minutes and the record's two-lane confirmation figures are the outage's, not the rule's.
2. **The freeze, read on the chain.** With lane 2 out, lanes 0 and 1 earned four credits each and then none for 609 chain blocks: the weight of the honest chain did not grow for 73 minutes while every block was ordered and paid. Nothing went wrong from it (one tip on every node throughout, no race), which is why it must be read as the minimum rule's liveness cost made exact, not as a pass: a race during the freeze would have been decided by the blue score, form A at unit work. The chain form's answer is the served band (a lane whose controller is under twice its floor leaves the minimum); this cut does not read it (section 7), and the panel's choice stands open.
3. **A lane returning at a decayed difficulty is a chip for a quarter of an hour, and the credit held.** Lane 2's difficulty had decayed from 0.98 to 0.014 of its reference in 73 minutes (the absolute controller's debt: the clock ahead of the count); back at two threads it sealed about one block a second across its two sealers, 67 of the next 60 chain blocks' blues, and the controller took seventeen minutes to lift it to 0.70. Under form A with the registered reference those blocks would each have weighed 0.014 (the unit of step 0's measure follows D / D_ref), under the unit they weigh one each and the credit capped them at the least lane plus four: the three counts rose 106 / 106 / 110 to 188 / 185 / 189 together. Also read: two sealers of one lane at a 0.014 difficulty race at every block (50 depth-1 reorganisations in the burst, the hash tie deciding), the DAG's parallel-block path exercised by a lane's own sealers rather than by latency.
4. **The slack's cost on honest lanes is a fifth of their blocks at S = 4.** Before the stop on 7412 the three lanes at one rate were credited 102 / 102 / 101 of 134 / 137 / 127 blue blocks (24, 26 and 20 percent uncredited); on 7413 the two honest lanes 24 and 41 of 201 and 214 (12 and 19 percent). This is weight the honest chain does not accrue (never a reordering, and the least lane always credits), and every bound of section 2 scales with S; the registration's S against the controllers' noise is a number for the review.
5. **The peering must come inside the first block interval, on both boxes.** 7412's lab box A nodes sealed for four minutes before the peering (the lab's two chains were started one after the other and 7413's lab box A nodes had to be moved off a port block another service holds, 8831 to 8835), so the peering merge made 40 red blocks and reorganisations of depth 14, against 7413's 17 and 9 at 110 seconds. ## 7. Caveats, plainly

- **The bound is on the WEIGHT, and the weight is one input to the ordering.** Theorems 1 to 3 bound what a lane's blocks can add to the blue work; they say nothing about the colouring (a chip lane's parallel blocks are blue or red by the k-cluster rule as any blocks are) or about the pay (every blue block is paid at its order, credited or not: a chip on one lane still earns that lane's subsidy, as the design's pay row says it should; 7413's lane 2 was paid 360 blocks' subsidy for a third of the weight). The chip-resistance hypothesis of the design's section 5 is "holds no authority over the ordering"; this step gives the weight half of it a proof and a measurement, and the withholding, balance and two-lane rows are still to be run.
- **The merging attacker's constant margin** (section 2 (a)): at most 2 (n - 1) S = 16 units over the public tip, not zero. What a 16-unit margin buys at a release is the withholding row's measurement, read against the merge-depth bound of 150 orders. the review's reading (F-809, PANEL-READING-5): at unit work 16 units is 16 orders, over the ladder's CONFIRMED depth of 3 by five times, so a party holding the majority of two lanes' hash rate can, on paper, release a merging branch that reorders a depth-3 confirmation; the k-cluster rule caps the branch that keeps the public lane's blocks blue at k private blocks (a longer branch reds them and freezes its own credit at the fork's count plus S, after which the honest chain outgrows it at one unit a least-lane block), so the depth that survives a release lies between k and 2 (n - 1) S orders plus the honest lanes' clipping lag. The "no confirmation reversed" of section 1 is a figure of the three-lane honest rows alone; the withholding row reads the depth, and the ladder's confirmation target is set from it, not from the honest rows.
- **The frozen state** (section 2 (b), section 5): a stopped lane of the minimum freezes the credit after S blocks of each other lane; the chain is then ordered by the count until the lane returns (7412's 73 minutes). This is the minimum rule's liveness consequence, accepted by VECTORWORK section 8 for the chain form, and the served band the chain form reads to leave a floor lane out of the minimum is NOT read here: a band read from the branch's own controllers lets a party drop a lane out of the minimum by waiting for its decay (an hour and a half at the lab's constants, an hour at the research chains' lag cap), a time escape the review lead's ruling (b9ff75f28: the band at the common ancestor) closes on the chain form and a per-block credit has no ancestor to read it at. The panel's choice: the band from the controller with a finality depth under the floor's decay time (Kaspa's finality rule, a tip whose selected chain lacks the node's finality point refused), or no band and the frozen state as the accepted outage cost. the review's reading (F-810, PANEL-READING-5): the chain is LIVE through the freeze, not stalled (7412: 609 chain blocks and 200 of the record's 300 transactions ordered and paid by two lanes, CONFIRMED at depth 3 in 22.9 s at the median, the two-lane rate's cost); what stops is the weight, so for the outage the head is chosen by the blue score, Kaspa's count, and the chip resistance at consensus is suspended for a lane's hash-rate transient (the returning lane's burst, step 0's finding). A lane can be silenced as well as lost (its few sealers stopped by a party that holds a chip on another lane), so the frozen state is an attack surface and not only an outage cost; the panel's default for the next design revision is the band with the finality depth, the frozen state standing as the recorded cost until that cut is read.
- **The slack is a registration constant, taken as k** (section 6 item 4): a fifth of the honest blocks uncredited at S = 4; a larger S credits more and loosens every bound in proportion.
- **A re-registration is unread.** A lane entering the minimum at a height enters at count zero and would freeze the others until it has caught up to within S; the labs register three lanes from the genesis and the case is not exercised. The other direction (F-811): a lane that LEAVES the minimum at a height (retired, or without a pay weight under F-166's breadth) has its blocks sealed below that height credited, when merged above it, against the other lanes alone, the rule reading the lanes in the minimum for the others and never for the block's own lane; bounded by S a lane as every credit is, and a row for the re-registration cut. The minimum is read at the block's own height through its selected parent (F-807, cured on the landing: the first cut read it by the block's own hash, which a block under verification does not yet have in the chain, so it answered for height zero there and for the true height on a recomputation; the labs' registration is active from zero, so their records are unchanged).
- **The restart row's two halves.** The stop was measured as briefed; the restart was by hand 73 minutes later with a decayed difficulty (section 6 item 1), a stronger transient than the brief's and not the brief's row; the record's two-lane confirmation figures are the outage's.
- **The lab's latency against a real network's, the null algorithm, two sealers a lane, the reachability walk, the acceptance record, settlement as the merge-depth bound, the hash tie:** step 0's caveats, unchanged and not repeated.
