## 2. The rule: the cadence a field of the gate entry, k and S from it

**The parameter.** params.IgneumChainDAGFrom's entry is now a pair, DAGGate{From, LaneSeconds}: the first height under the ordering (zero off, as before) and the LANE CADENCE, the seconds a lane's controller holds between the lane's blocks from the gate height, in place of the registration's target. Zero keeps the registration's (the steps 0 and 1 labs: 7411 to 7413 at the registration's 12 s). The engine reads it through one function, dagLaneTarget(registered, height): the entry's cadence where the ordering is in force at the height and the entry names one, else the registration's target; vector.go reads that function at the two places the controller reads a target (the folded lag's per-block subtraction and the ASERT parameters at the child's height). From the cadence on, the controller reads the FOLDED lag (revision 10's form) rather than the anchored one, so a cadence from a later gate height changes the slope of the lag and not the difficulty at the landing check (the anchored form would re-read the whole history at the new target and jump); the two agree wherever the cadence is the registration's and the cap was never reached, so the steps 0 and 1 chains read as before. The backend hands the entry to the engine (SetDAGFrom(from, laneSeconds)); igneum_dagHead carries laneSeconds beside k. The lab's proof that the parameter is read from the ENTRY and not the registration: both chains registered the lanes at target 12 (the same registration as steps 0 and 1) and the nodes held them at 2.05 s and 1.22 s a merged block.

**The k.** The paper's formula as the engine computes it (dag.K, Kaspa's calculate_ghostdag_k): k is the least integer with P[Poisson(x) > k] < delta, x = 2 D lambda, with D the delay bound of 2 seconds (the design's target for the geographic row), delta = 0.01 (Kaspa's GHOSTDAG_TAIL_DELTA) and lambda the MERGED block rate, the lane count over the cadence (DAGKFor(lanes, laneSeconds)). The inputs and the outputs: three lanes at 12 s, lambda = 0.25 a second, x = 1, k = 4 (the steps 0 and 1 figure: P[N > 3] = 1.9 percent, P[N > 4] = 0.37 percent); at 6 s, lambda = 0.5, x = 2, k = 6 (P[N > 5] = 1.66 percent, P[N > 6] = 0.45 percent); at 3 s, lambda = 1, x = 4, k = 9 (P[N > 8] = 2.14 percent, P[N > 9] = 0.81 percent). A chain of no lanes under the landing check reads the design's three lanes (k = 4 as before). TestDAGKFollowsTheCadence reads the table; TestDAGLaneCadenceFromTheEntry the target before and from the landing check, with and without a cadence, on and off the gate.

**The slack S = k.** Step 1 took S = k = 4 as a constant (DAGCreditSlack); this cut makes S the k in force (dagLookup.CreditSlack returns the engine's k), one anticone's worth of one lane's blocks at the rate, so the bound of step 1's section 2 reads 1/3 + 2 S / (3 T) with S = 6 at 6 s (under 1/3 + 0.01 from T = 400) and S = 9 at 3 s (from T = 600). 2. **Peer within the first block interval and the peering merge disappears.** 7422's eight nodes were peered 26 s after the start (the first block takes the hash rate ~12 s at the reference, and the two boxes were started together from the Mac): 21 chain blocks in the first minute, no red. 7423 (16 s to the peering, its reference a quarter) read one red at chain block 13. ## 7. Caveats, plainly

- **The lab's latency against a real network's, by the paper's model.** The two boxes see each other in 0.6 ms; the design's D is 2 s and a real wider internet 100 ms and more between halls. Under the GHOSTDAG model two blocks are parallel when they are made within the propagation delay of each other: the expected number of blocks parallel to a given one is 2 D lambda, so the fraction of blocks with a parallel block is about 1 - e^(-2 D lambda). At the lab's 0.6 ms that is 0.06 percent at 2 s a block and 0.12 at 1 s (the lab read 0.3 and 0.9 percent: the sealers' 1 s recommit and the two sealers of one lane); at a 100 ms wider internet it is 9.5 percent at 2 s a block and 18 percent at 1 s, and at the design's D of 2 s it is 86 and 98 percent (F-813: an earlier draft wrote 63 and 86, the pair for 4 s and 2 s a block). Those parallel blocks are MERGED, blue, ordered and paid, not orphaned: a block is red only when its anticone exceeds k, and k was chosen so that the probability of more than k blocks within 2 D at D = 2 s is under 1 percent (P[Poisson(2) > 6] = 0.45 percent at 6 s a lane; P[Poisson(4) > 9] = 0.81 percent at 3 s); at D = 100 ms the model's red rate is P[Poisson(0.1) > 6], under 10^-9, and P[Poisson(0.2) > 9], under 10^-11. What a 100 ms network does cost by the model is the confirmation: a parallel block's anticone must settle before the k-cluster can place it, so the depth-3 clocks above lengthen by of the order of the delay times the parallel fraction (tens of milliseconds at 100 ms, seconds at D = 2 s); a chain WITHOUT the ordering would orphan the parallel fraction outright (10 to 18 percent at 100 ms). None of this is measured here: the lab's zero reds are the lab's latency, and the model's own numbers are the claim until the continental row (the design's G2) is run.
- **The settlement bound is in orders, not seconds.** DAGMergeDepth = 150 orders was the design's 10 minutes at 4 s a block; at 2 s it is 5 minutes and at 1 s 2.5 (7422 read 387 s median because the controllers were slower than 2 s for the first half of the run). Whether the bound should follow the cadence (300 orders at 6 s a lane, 600 at 3 s, to keep the 10 minutes of the design) or stay at 150 is a choice for the review: this cut leaves it at 150 and reports the time it gives. THE PANEL'S CHOICE (F-818): the bound stays a COUNT IN ORDERS and is not re-stated in seconds. A bound in seconds would read the sealers' timestamps (the sampled window's timestamps are the manipulation VECTORWORK.md section 7 (ii) names as uncovered), and the thing the bound holds off is a branch measured in blocks: a party at a fraction q of the merged rate makes 150 q / (1 - q) blocks while the honest lanes make 150 orders, at any rate, so the count's security is rate-free in the papers' model and only its seconds halve with the rate. The design's 10 minutes were the seconds of 150 orders at 4 s a block, not a target in seconds. 150 orders stands as the lab's figure (5 minutes at 6 s a lane); the count of record is set by the withholding row's measured reach (the fifth reading's F-809), not by the clock, and 300 is not chosen for the clock's sake. The pool's anchor window (64 and 150 orders) is read in seconds at the pool step only for the wallet's proving time against it (64 orders are about two minutes at 2 s a block; the prove is 32 to 37 ms on eight cores and under 200 ms on one, so the window holds).
