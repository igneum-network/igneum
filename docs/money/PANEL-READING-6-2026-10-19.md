## 1. The k, from the formula (the review lead, the security review)

The code (dagform.go DAGKFor, dag.K) computes Kaspa's calculate_ghostdag_k: the least k with P[Poisson(x) > k] < delta at x = 2 D lambda, D = 2 s, delta = 0.01, lambda the lane count over the cadence. The panel recomputed the tails: three lanes at 12 s, x = 1, P[N > 3] = 1.90 percent, P[N > 4] = 0.37: k = 4; at 6 s, x = 2, P[N > 5] = 1.66, P[N > 6] = 0.45: k = 6; at 3 s, x = 4, P[N > 8] = 2.14, P[N > 9] = 0.81: k = 9. The page's table is right and TestDAGKFollowsTheCadence reads it. S = k is one anticone's worth of one lane's blocks at the rate, as step 1 defined it; CreditSlack returns the k in force.

**Is D = 2 s honest (F-814)?** It is the design's stated TARGET for the geographic row (ORDERING-DESIGN section 2.2 item 1: "a TARGET for the geographic row, not a figure"), and the page names it as such in section 2; what is measured is p95 1,052 ms at the edge in one hall and the continental row (DEVNET.md G2) has not been run. The formula is the paper's and is not the question; its input is. The panel's sensitivity table, written onto the page's section 7:

| each lane at | D = 0.5 s | D = 1 s | D = 2 s (the design's) | D = 4 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 s (x = 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 / 2) | k = 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 |
| 6 s (x = 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4) | k = 3 | 4 | **6** | 9 |
| 3 s (x = 1 / 2 / 4 / 8) | k = 4 | 6 | 9 | 15 |

The two failure directions, both stated on the page now: a k set for a D smaller than the real one reds honest blocks (k = 6 at a real D of 4 s reds 11 percent of them by the model, P[Poisson(4) > 6] = 0.111; reds are unpaid, so the lane's income and the credit's fairness go with them); a k set for a D larger than the real one widens every anticone and the merging attacker's margin 2 (n - 1) S for nothing (k = 6 at a real D of 0.5 s is twice the 12 units D needs). The ruling: k = 6 is the k for a D of 2 s that has not been measured; the continental row runs before the research chain's gate entry is set and the entry's k is re-derived from the measured D. The rate of record is not in question by this: at 6 s a lane every D in the table gives a k between 3 and 9, and the costs and the confirmation clocks do not move with k.

**The k's provenance in the code (F-815, the security review).** SetDAGFrom takes the lane count from vectorMenu().Count(), an atomic load of the registry's state at the node's start, and sets k and S once. On a chain whose lane count changes (a registration or a succession) a node started before the change and one started after would hold different k and colour the same DAG differently: a consensus split. Not reached by anything of record: the five labs hold three lanes throughout and every chain of record is off the gate (section 6), and the formula's output is the same for the labs whether the menu answers three or nothing (dagDesignLanes). Listed for the research chain's cut, not cured here because the cure is a design choice the panel does not make for the author: either k a pinned field of the gate entry (DAGGate{From, LaneSeconds, K}) with DAGKFor a test on the entry, or the lane count read at the gate height; in both SetDAGFrom stops reading the live menu. The lockless read of e.dag.k in CreditSlack under a colouring that copied it under the lock is consistent with k set once and is not a fault.

## 2. The settlement bound in orders against seconds (the review lead; F-809's line, F-818)

The page's section 7 put the choice to the review: keep DAGMergeDepth at 150 orders (5 minutes at 2 s a block, 2.5 at 1 s) or scale it with the cadence to keep the design's 10 minutes. The panel's choice: the bound stays a COUNT IN ORDERS, and 150 stands as the lab's figure until the withholding row sets the count of record. The reasons, on the page: (i) a bound in seconds reads the sealers' timestamps, and the sampled window's timestamps are the manipulation VECTORWORK.md section 7 (ii) names as uncovered; (ii) what the bound holds off is a branch measured in blocks, and in the papers' model a party at a fraction q of the merged rate makes 150 q / (1 - q) blocks while the honest lanes make 150 orders at any rate, so the count's security is rate-free and only its seconds halve with the rate; (iii) the design's 10 minutes were the seconds of 150 orders at 4 s a block, a derived figure and not a target. The fifth reading's F-809 stands over this: the count of record, the confirmation depth and the pool's anchor window are set from the withholding row's measured reach. The anchor window is read in seconds at the pool step only for the wallet's proving time against it (64 orders about two minutes at 2 s a block; the prove 32 to 37 ms on eight cores, under 200 ms on one).

- The N100's reader share at 1 s a block: 19 to 32 percent of one core at the measured factor of 2, 38 to 64 at a factor of 4, before the pool's 32 proofs a block (11.3 percent, measured on the N100) and the lane's real check (ESTIMATE up to 25 percent at 1 s): 31 to 43 percent with the pool at the factor of 2, 49 to 75 at 4, up to one whole core with the lane check's worst estimate. - The k against an unmeasured D (F-814): at 3 s a lane the table reads k = 4 at D = 0.5 s, 6 at 1 s, 9 at 2 s, 15 at 4 s. k = 9 at a real D of 4 s reds 28 percent of honest blocks by the model (P[Poisson(8) > 9] = 0.283, against 11 percent for k = 6 at 6 s a lane); at a real D of 1 s it carries a margin of 36 orders where 24 would do. The faster rate is more sensitive to D in both directions, which is why the continental row comes before the landing check entry.

**The measurement on the real network that would send it back to 2 s a block (6 s a lane).** Two readings on the research chain's own nodes across the halls, under the lab clock rule's minimum (the first thousand merged blocks after the gate and after the peering, and the withholding row's one release):

1. The steady-state red rate at k = 9 over the first thousand blues after the peering, read by igneum_dag* on every node: the design's line is 2 percent. The model's figure at 100 ms is nil and at D = 2 s 0.8 percent; a measured rate over 2 percent means the real D is above the design's target, and the cure is first the k (re-derived from the measured D) and, where the re-derived k at 1 s a block exceeds 9, the rate back to 2 s a block (k = 6 at D = 2 s).
2. The withholding row's reach at k = 9 (a private branch of one lane, and the merging two-lane branch, released against form B's credit): the depth in orders at which a confirmed transaction is reversed, measured against the rows' depth 3 and 6.

**The condition under which the panel would refuse 3 s a lane**, written now so that the row decides and not the author: a steady-state red rate over 2 percent on the research chain at k = 9, or a withholding reach at k = 9 that reverses a transaction confirmed at depth 3 in the row, is a FAIL of the rate. The cure is not automatic: the reach is a function of k and S, not of the clock, so the rate goes back to 2 s a block only where the same row at k = 6 keeps depth 3 inside the reach; otherwise the cure is the confirmation depth (F-809's line), at either rate, and the rate stands. A third refusal: the N100 reader on the research chain's mesh above one core at 1 s a block with the pool's 32 proofs a block, read on the node (threshold 2's form), sends the rate to 2 s regardless of the rows.

**What the panel keeps from its own reading.** 6 s a lane remains the panel's figure of safety on the costs and the N100's margin, and the panel would have chosen it; the founder's 3 s is inside the measured rows at the lab's latency and inside the design's confirmation target by the widest margin of the ladder, and the risks above are the model's until the two readings are taken. The gate entry's cadence field carries 3 s as the research chain's value in the next cut (the step 4 lab at 3 s a lane, and the research chain's entry {From: the review lead's height, LaneSeconds: 3} when it is cut); the live chains stay at zero until then.
