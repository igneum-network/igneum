## 1. The decision

Fast confirmation on Igneum means this, in numbers: a shielded or public transfer broadcast to the network is included in a block within 4 seconds at the median, is CONFIRMED (its block is in the blue set of the ordering with enough ordered work above it that reversing it would need the network's majority for longer than the attacker's patience, the threshold the ladder measures) within 12 seconds at the median and 30 seconds at the 95th percentile, and is SETTLED (past the merge-depth bound, so that no block any node will accept can reorder it) within 10 minutes; the vault's delays are counted in the ordering's clock and never shortened by a reorganisation. Beside it: Kaspa runs at 10 blocks a second since the Crescendo activation of May 2025 (rusty-kaspa README; the activation at DAA score 110,165,000, "Roughly 2025-05-05 1500 UTC", consensus/core/src/config/params.rs) and states a full confirmation of about 10 seconds on average (kaspa.org, the home page's wording as indexed; the features page says transactions that confirm after an hour on Bitcoin "confirm in seconds on Kaspa"), with its finality depth 12 hours and its merge-depth bound one hour (FINALITY_DURATION 43,200 s, MERGE_DEPTH_DURATION 3,600 s, consensus/core/src/config/constants.rs); Monero's block target is 120 seconds and a received output is spendable after 10 blocks, about 20 minutes (DIFFICULTY_TARGET_V2 120, CRYPTONOTE_DEFAULT_TX_SPENDABLE_AGE 10, src/cryptonote_config.h); Zcash's block spacing is 75 seconds since Blossom (ZIP 208), and a wallet of record spends received funds after 10 confirmations, about 12.5 minutes (Zodl support). Igneum today seals one chain at a 12-second target across three lanes (docs/build/RUN-A-SEALER.md: 11.5 to 13 seconds a block measured), and the same page states the bound a party alone on a served lane can reach, twelve blocks, about two and a half minutes, which is why the wallet says "submitted, included, work on top" and never "final". This stage comes after the pool and the vault because the ordering changes what "height" means for every rule the pool and the vault are gated and clocked by (section 3), and a private pool whose nullifier set, anchors and delays were designed on a single chain must be re-read under parallel blocks (section 4) before anything moves; designing the pool on the DAG first would mean measuring two unmeasured constructions at once, which ADR-0001's stop condition refuses.

## 2. The construction

### 2.1 Kaspa's ordering, as published

What the papers state (their abstracts, read on the ePrint pages; the theorems APPROXIMATE from memory where marked):

- PHANTOM and GHOSTDAG (Sompolinsky, Wyborski, Zohar; ePrint 2018/104, 17 revisions, the last 10 November 2021; AFT 2021). The abstract: PHANTOM generalises Nakamoto's chain to a block DAG; a block references every tip of the DAG its miner knows, so blocks created in parallel are all kept and none is orphaned; a tunable parameter k lets the system tolerate concurrently created blocks and so raises the block rate past the propagation delay; PHANTOM's selection of a "k-cluster" is NP-hard, so GHOSTDAG, a greedy approximation, is proposed and the paper proves its block ordering irreversible up to an exponentially negligible factor. APPROXIMATE, the body: the honest blocks form a k-cluster, a set in which no block has more than k blocks of the set in its anticone (neither in its past nor its future); k is chosen from the network delay D, the block rate lambda and a tolerance delta so that the probability that more than k honest blocks are created within one delay is below delta; the security assumption is that the honest miners hold a majority of the hash rate and that the delay is bounded by D; confirmation time is a function of D and the adversary's share, not of the block interval, so a higher rate shortens the wait to a confirmation at a given confidence.
- DAG KNIGHT (Sompolinsky, Sutton; ePrint 2022/1494, revised 24 February 2023). The abstract: a permissionless protocol with no a priori in-protocol bound over latency, the first of its kind, which tolerates a corruption of up to 50 percent of the hash rate; to avoid the Pass and Shi impossibility result, the client specifies locally an upper bound over the maximum recent adversarial latency; the protocol evolves PHANTOM. APPROXIMATE, the body: instead of a fixed k, the ordering searches over k for the smallest k whose k-cluster covers the majority of the DAG's work, so that confirmation times track the latency the network actually has and a healthy network confirms faster than a worst-case bound would allow.

What the implementation of record does (rusty-kaspa, consensus/src/processes/ghostdag/protocol.rs, read on the file): the selected parent is the parent with the greatest blue work; the block's mergeset (its past minus the selected parent's past) is walked in a consensus-agreed topological order; a candidate is blue if (i) its anticone within the blue set holds at most k blocks and (ii) no blue block's blue anticone would exceed k by its addition, both checked by walking the selected-parent chain and counting, with a quick rejection once the block already holds k + 1 mergeset blues; otherwise red; blue score is the selected parent's plus the count of mergeset blues; blue work is the selected parent's plus the work of each mergeset blue computed from its difficulty bits. The ordering is the selected chain with each block's mergeset in that order. The parameters at 10 blocks a second (consensus/core/src/config/bps.rs and constants.rs): k = 124 from the table calculate_ghostdag_k(2 D lambda, delta) with D = NETWORK_DELAY_BOUND 5 s and delta = GHOSTDAG_TAIL_DELTA 0.01; at most 16 parents a block "to preserve processing performance"; the mergeset limited to 248; the merge-depth bound 3,600 s; finality 43,200 s; pruning 108,000 s; coinbase maturity 100 s; the difficulty window 2,641 s sampled every 4 s. Kaspa's node at 10 blocks a second asks for 16 GB of memory, a 256 GB SSD and about 40 Mbit/s (docs/crescendo-guide.md). Kaspa's heights are not heights: its activations and maturities are gated by DAA score, a count of blue blocks in a block's past, and its features page targets 25 to 40 millisecond blocks under DAG KNIGHT.

### 2.2 What Igneum keeps and what changes

**Kept.** The three lanes and their kernels; each lane's own difficulty controller (the anchored relative ASERT of node/consensus/igneum/difficulty/asert.go, per lane under the vector form); the share lane (shares carried in the block, the payout list derived from them, node/consensus/igneum/mineshares.go); the 64-block pay and 256-block floor accounts and the minimum pay rule (docs/consensus/WHOLEWORK.md section 11); the sponsored lane's escrow and windows; the registry, the succession and the revision cadence; the fee rule and the burn; the sortition's bond, the open seat, the rounds and the lot; the shielded pool of ADR-0001 section 4.3 unchanged in its statement; the issuance's calendar.

**Changed, one at a time.**

1. **The 12-second single chain becomes a block DAG at a target of one block every 4 seconds across all lanes, each lane at a 12-second target of its own.** Why 4 seconds and not Kaspa's 100 milliseconds: an Igneum block is not a Kaspa block. Its validation carries a lane's proof-of-work check that is GPU work replayed on the CPU, up to 256 carried shares each needing one hash on the lane's twin document, the derived payout list, the EVM's execution and, after stage one, the pool's proofs at 3.1 ms a spend as the node pays it on one EPYC core, 6 to 30 ms ESTIMATE on the N100 class (docs/money/STAGE1-JOIN-2026-10-18.md section 1 row 2); the minimum node's whole-block gate is 250 ms at p99 (docs/reliability/DEVNET.md G1). At one block a second that gate alone is a quarter of a core continuously and the headers and share entries are about 60 GB a year unpruned (section 6); at one block every 4 seconds it is a sixteenth of a core and about 16 GB a year, within the 128 GB SSD with the pool's state beside it. The measured propagation is p95 1,052 ms at the edge in one hall and not yet read across continents (DEVNET.md G2); with a delay bound D of 2 seconds (a TARGET for the geographic row, not a figure) and lambda of 0.25 a second, 2 D lambda = 1, and the k the Kaspa table would give is small, APPROXIMATE 4 to 6 against delta 0.01, so the DAG is thin and the anticone a transaction must wait out is short. The rate is a registration constant of the ladder's first step, with 2 seconds the alternative to read beside it.
2. **The lanes as parallel block streams.** Each lane produces its own blocks at its own difficulty under its own controller, as today; every block references every tip it knows, of any lane; one ordering merges them. Is this sound under the papers' model? In part. The k-cluster argument counts BLOCKS in an anticone and assumes they arrive as a Poisson process at rate lambda under a delay D; the sum of three independent Poisson streams is a Poisson stream at the summed rate, so a single global k over the total rate is inside the papers' model, and nothing in the colouring (blue or red) needs to know a block's lane. What is NOT inside the model is the WEIGHT. The papers' selected parent is the one with the greatest blue work, work being additive over blocks from their difficulty bits, and the security theorem is stated for a majority of that one scalar (APPROXIMATE). Igneum's fork choice does not use one scalar: from the fork-weight gate its first key is the MINIMUM over the served lanes of the branch's normalised work, so that a party holding one or two lanes holds no fork authority (node/consensus/igneum/firstseen.go; docs/consensus/VECTORWORK.md section 2, the coverage rule). A minimum is not additive, and the GHOSTDAG proof needs an additive blue work. This page therefore states two forms and chooses neither until the ladder's first step reads them: FORM A, additive normalised work, blue work = the sum over mergeset blues of D(b) / D_ref(lane of b), which is the papers' metric with each lane's block counted as one target block at its reference; it is sound in the papers' model as far as the papers' proof carries, and it defends against a one-lane specialist (a third of the weight at most, since the lane's controller holds its block rate) but NOT against a two-lane specialist (two thirds), the case the minimum rule was written for. FORM B, the minimum carried into the DAG: the selected parent chosen by the lexicographic measure (first the minimum over served lanes of the normalised blue work in the candidate's past above the common ancestor, then the additive sum), with the served band read at the ancestor exactly as firstseen.go reads it. Form B keeps today's defence and is a DEPARTURE from the papers needing its own argument: the measure is not monotone in added blocks of one lane, the selected-parent choice can flip when a thin lane's block arrives late, and no published proof covers it. The honest statement: the lanes compose with the DAG's colouring; they compose with the DAG's weight only under form A, and form B is a research question of this stage, not an inheritance from Kaspa.
3. **The fork choice** becomes the selected chain of the ordering: a node's tip is the virtual block whose parents are all its tips, and the canonical ordering is the virtual block's. First-seen at equal height (firstseen.go, between single siblings) has no object in a DAG, since both siblings are kept; the lot tie-break survives as the deterministic tie among equal blue work (Kaspa breaks such ties by hash, APPROXIMATE; Igneum keeps the sealer's registered draw of VECTORWORK.md revision 9, which no sealer can grind). The withholding rule's length condition L = 4k blocks has no object either: a withheld branch released late is red by the k-cluster rule and adds nothing (VECTORWORK.md (e) WITHHOLDING reads the same outcome under the coverage rule).
4. **Finality** has three lines, each a number of the ladder: CONFIRMED at the client's confidence (DAG KNIGHT's form: the wallet chooses its tolerated latency, the node reports the probability bound; under GHOSTDAG with fixed k the node reports blue score above the block); SETTLED at the merge-depth bound (a block that would merge a chain deeper than the bound is refused, so the ordering below it cannot change on any node that follows the rule; Kaspa's 3,600 s; this page's target 10 minutes, 150 blue blocks at the target rate, to be set from the measured D and the attacks of section 5); PRUNABLE at the finality depth (Kaspa's 12 hours), the depth below which a node may drop bodies and keep headers, the proofs of the pool among the bodies (ADR-0001 section 4.6's pruned-sync path).

## 3. Every consensus rule it touches

Kaspa gates its activations and maturities by DAA score, the count of blue blocks in a block's past along the selected chain, which is monotone, deterministic and the same on every node (its Crescendo activation is a DAA score). Igneum takes the same object and calls it the ORDER: a block's order is its blue score; the ordering's index of a block (its position in the virtual block's ordering) is a second number that the readers use and no consensus rule gates on, since it moves until the merge-depth bound. ## 4. The private pool under parallel blocks

This section decides whether the two constructions compose on paper. The pool of ADR-0001 section 4.3 is unchanged in its statement; what changes is the chain it writes to.

**The nullifier set when two parallel blocks spend the same note.** Two blocks in each other's anticone can each carry a transfer publishing the same nullifier nf. The rule: the ordering decides; the first of the two in the ordering is accepted and inserts nf into the nullifier set; the second is NOT accepted (its proof is valid, its nullifier is already in the set as the state stands at its ordered position), its block stays valid, and no fee is paid for it because it never executed. The proof obligation, which the ladder's step 4 discharges by reading and not by assumption: (i) the ordering is a deterministic function of the DAG, so every node accepts the same one (the papers' property, kept by the implementation's consensus-agreed mergeset order); (ii) below the merge-depth bound the ordering cannot change, so the winner is settled; (iii) above it the winner can change while the two blocks' relative order can change, which is the papers' reversal probability and nothing new; (iv) the losing transfer is not a double spend and not a fault, it is the same note spent once. What is new and must be costed: a losing transfer occupies block space and pays nothing, and the sealers of parallel blocks cannot see each other's mempools in time, so one note can fill one slot in every block of an anticone at no cost to its owner; the bound is the anticone's size (at most k + 1 blues plus reds) per note per 4 seconds, and the cell to run is a flood of self-conflicting transfers against the block budget of section 6. The mitigation if the cell fails: the sealer's inclusion of a shielded transfer is charged a base fee from the sealer's own tip at acceptance of the block (a rule of this chain, not Kaspa's), so that a sealer paces conflicting transfers; named as an option, not chosen.

**The Merkle root a prover commits to when blocks are parallel.** A spend proves membership under an anchor (a root of the commitment tree). On the DAG the tree is appended in the ORDER, blue and red blocks alike, so the tree's state at a block is the state after its mergeset, and the anchor of a block near the tip changes when the ordering changes above the merge-depth bound. The rule: the anchor named in a spend must be the root of the tree at a selected-chain block in the past of the block that carries the spend, and that block must be within the ANCHOR WINDOW in order behind the carrying block (Zcash accepts any past anchor on its chain, ZIP 209's form cited in ADR-0001 for the turnstile; Igneum bounds the window so a node keeps a ring of roots and not every root); the node keeps the roots of the last W selected-chain blocks, W a registration constant with the merge-depth bound as its natural size (150 at the target rate) and 64 the smaller reading. How many blocks back an anchor may be: at least the settled depth, since a root taken at the tip may be of a block that turns red or moves, in which case the tree's order of leaves below the root differs and the proof fails; a root taken at a block below the merge-depth bound is settled and its leaves' positions final. What the wallet does: it takes the anchor of the selected-chain block at the SETTLED line (10 minutes behind the tip at the target) when the note it spends is older than that, which is the common case and costs nothing in wait since the proof is made against a settled root; for a note received inside the last 10 minutes it either waits for the settled line or proves against a tip-near anchor and accepts that a reordering voids the proof and the wallet must re-prove (5 to 30 seconds on a PC, ADR-0001 section 4.5). The wallet says which it did. The ledger of roots per block is a new node cost (32 bytes a block, trivial) and the anchor window's size is the one constant this section leaves to the ladder.

**The vault's delays, measured in the ordering's clock, not in time and not in ordered blocks.** A vault withdrawal waits a delay the owner chose; a recovery waits a longer one. Ordered blocks are the wrong unit (the DAG's width varies and red blocks count), and the block timestamp is the wrong unit (a sealer's field). The unit is the ORDER, the blue score along the selected chain, which the controllers hold at the target rate so that a delay of one day is 21,600 in order, and a wallet shows it in the person's local time by the rate; a reorganisation above the merge-depth bound cannot shorten a delay, since a withdrawal's announcement is a transaction accepted at an order and its completion needs an order at least the delay later, both read on the same monotone number; if the announcement's block turns red and its transaction is re-accepted in a later block, the delay runs from the LATER order, so a reorganisation can only lengthen it. The vault's pending state, visible in the wallet, carries the announcement's order and the earliest completing order.

**Reorganisation behaviour and what a recipient sees.** Three words, and the wallet's existing rule (nothing on screen calls a transaction final, ADR-0001 section 8) holds: PENDING, the transfer is in a block in the recipient's node's DAG but below the confirmation threshold of section 2.2 item 4, and it may move or lose a nullifier race; CONFIRMED, above the threshold, where the probability of reversal is the papers' bound at the wallet's chosen confidence; SETTLED, past the merge-depth bound, where no node following the rule accepts a block that reorders it. A received note is spendable by the wallet at CONFIRMED against a tip-near anchor and at SETTLED against a settled anchor, and the wallet labels which; the public side's balances follow the same three words.

**What is unknown, plainly.** (1) Whether the pool's state transition under Kaspa's merged-past form keeps the turnstile exact when a block's accepted set differs from its carried set (the turnstile counts accepted transfers only; a reading, not a proof). (2) The anchor window's size against the real reordering depth, a measurement. (3) The free-slot flood of self-conflicting transfers against the block budget, a measurement, with the sealer-paid fee as the fallback. (4) The vault's running total per period under the order clock when the period boundary falls inside an anticone (two withdrawals in parallel blocks, each under the limit, together over it: the ordering accepts the first and refuses the second by the same rule as the nullifier, and the proof obligation is the same (i) to (iv)). (5) Whether proving against a settled anchor, the common case, makes the confirmation target of section 1 meaningless for the pool: a shielded transfer confirms in 12 seconds only if its note was received more than 10 minutes earlier; a note received and re-spent inside 10 minutes confirms in 12 seconds only on the tip-near path with its re-prove risk. This page states it and does not hide it: for the pool, "confirms in seconds" is a property of transfers of settled notes, and the ladder measures the fraction of transfers that are such. The composition verdict on paper: the two constructions compose, with the conflict rule and the anchor rule above and two measurements (2 and 3) that could still refuse it; nothing in the pool's cryptography changes, and nothing in the ordering's proof is weakened by the pool, since a shielded transfer is to the ordering what any transaction is.

## 5. Security

**What the papers prove and under what assumptions.** GHOSTDAG: the ordering is irreversible up to an exponentially negligible factor (the abstract), under, APPROXIMATE, an honest majority of the hash rate, a delay bounded by D known in advance, and k set from D, lambda and delta; the probability that honest blocks fall outside the k-cluster is delta per the implementation's comment ("Desired upper bound on the probability of anticones larger than k", constants.rs). DAG KNIGHT: tolerance of a corruption of up to 50 percent of the hash rate with no in-protocol latency bound, the client naming its own bound on recent adversarial latency (the abstract). Neither paper states anything about blocks of unequal difficulty from distinct hardware classes; their miners are one population at one difficulty, and that is the one assumption Igneum does not meet.

**What changes with heterogeneous lanes.** The honest majority becomes a vector. Under form A (additive normalised work) the theorem's majority is the majority of the sum, and a party holding two lanes outright holds two thirds of it: the papers' bound holds and the attacker it bounds is the one the lanes were built to deny, so form A is sound and insufficient. Under form B (the minimum first) the attacker holding two lanes holds nothing on the first key, as today, and the proof is absent. There is a third thing the lanes change: the delay D is not one number. A lane whose blocks are heavier to verify (the anvil lane's programme check) propagates slower than the raster lane's, so the effective D is the slowest lane's, and k must be set from it; the ladder's first step measures D per lane.

**The attacks to run, each a row with its command, its draws and its finding.**

- Withholding: a private DAG of one lane's blocks released at a chosen moment, against form A and form B; the papers' prediction is that the released blocks are red and add nothing; the lane form's prediction is read against it.
- Balance attacks across lanes: an attacker splitting work across two lanes to keep two honest sub-DAGs apart (the balance attack is published against GHOST-family rules, APPROXIMATE from memory of the literature; the DAG form's exposure is measured, not assumed).
- A chip taking one lane: a party at ten times a card's rate on one lane; the controller lifts the lane's difficulty, the lane's blue blocks stay at one every 12 seconds, the party holds a third of the additive weight and all of that lane's blocks; the measure under form A and under form B, and the pay rule's reading of the party's standing.
- A chip taking two lanes: the same at two lanes; the row that decides between form A and form B.
- Latency games: an attacker's nodes delaying their forwarding of one lane's blocks to make them red, against the DAG KNIGHT form where the client's latency bound absorbs it and the GHOSTDAG form where k must; and a sealer timing its block to land just inside an anticone to win a nullifier race for its own transfer.
- The self-conflicting flood of section 4 against the block budget.
- Difficulty manipulation of a lane's controller through the sampled window's timestamps (VECTORWORK.md section 7 (ii) names it as uncovered today).

**The chip-resistance claim at the consensus level, stated as a hypothesis to test and not as a property.** Hypothesis: on a block DAG ordered by form B, a party that can produce one or two lanes' blocks at any rate, at any cost, holds no authority over the ordering and earns on the pay rule no more than its weakest lane's standing. It is tested by the four rows above and it is not a property of this page; today's single-chain claim of RUN-A-SEALER.md rests on operators keeping every served lane sealed by more than one party, and that condition carries to the DAG unchanged.

## 6. The costs

At one block a second (the alternative) the headers and shares alone are 60 to 240 GB a year and the sustained verification 10 to 25 percent of a core before the pool, which is why 4 seconds is the figure and 1 second is not.

## 7. The ladder on the research chain

Each step ends in a record page with the chain, the order range, the node build, the commands and the figures, re-runnable by anyone with the repository. Nothing moves to the research chain (7358) before the throwaway steps pass, and nothing moves to the public test network from this ladder at all.

0. **A throwaway chain with the ordering alone and no pool.** Three lanes on the existing kernels at one block every 4 seconds, two sealers a lane on two boxes and two readers, no shares, no payout lists, the EVM carrying plain transfers; form A as the weight. Evidence: every node's ordering hash at every order equal; the measured D per lane; the red-block fraction; the reordering depth distribution over 24 hours; the k implied by the measured D at delta 0.01; the verification time per block on the N100-class stand-in against 250 ms; the headers' and bodies' bytes a day.
1. **Form B beside form A.** The same chain with the minimum-first measure; the two-lane specialist row (section 5) against both; the thin-lane late-arrival flip counted. Evidence: the rows; the decision between the forms written as a finding, or the finding that neither is sound enough and this stage stops.
2. **The share lane and the windows in order.** Shares with the parent-by-hash, the once-paid rule, the pay and floor accounts in order, red blocks unpaid; the ledger's tool on the DAG. Evidence: the S2 whole-list reconciliation to the wei over 1,000 blocks; the "carried later, unpaid" column exact; the subsidy per epoch equal to the schedule over 10,240 blue blocks.
3. **The issuance, the registry and the gates in order.** An epoch boundary crossed, a succession activated at an order with both forms' blocks in the anticone around it, the reveal anchor on the selected chain, the sponsored lane's window settled once. Evidence: the two-replay comparison of the issuance across the boundary; the F-730 lab form repeated in order.
4. **The pool on the DAG (after stage one of ADR-0001 has passed on the single chain).** The conflicting-nullifier cell (two parallel blocks, the same note), the self-conflicting flood against the budget, the anchor window at 64 and at 150, the re-prove path. Evidence: the accepted set equal on every node; the flood's cost per block; the fraction of transfers that proved against a settled anchor.
5. **The vault's clock in order.** A withdrawal announced in a block that turns red, re-accepted later, its delay lengthened and never shortened; two withdrawals in an anticone against the period limit. Evidence: the pending state exact after each case.
6. **The minimum node.** Steps 0 to 5's chain synced from genesis on the N100-class machine with pruning below the finality depth; the G1 and G2 gates read on it. Evidence: p99 per block; the disk after a week; the pruned sync's hours.
7. **The research chain.** The DAG from a gated order on 7358 with every node of record on the version, the readers and the public RPC serving the ordered index with the DAG fields, the record pages quoting orders and hashes.

**The stop condition.** This stays research, with no weakened claim and nothing on the public test network, if any of these holds at the end of the ladder: the composition with the pool is unmeasured (step 4 not run or its two measurements refusing the rule); a cost over the minimum node's budget (G1 over 250 ms at p99, the SSD not holding a year with pruning, the link over its ceiling at the stated transfer rate); neither form A nor form B surviving step 1; or ADR-0001's own verdict unchanged, in which case there is no pool to order and this page waits.

## 8. What is not claimed

Not claimed: that Igneum confirms in seconds; that the lanes compose with Kaspa's security proof (they compose with its colouring and, under form A, with its weight, on paper); that a chip cannot take the ordering (a hypothesis of section 5); that the pool's nullifier rule is settled (two measurements stand before it); that any figure in section 6 is measured; that Kaspa's 10 seconds is comparable to this page's 12, since its blocks and this chain's differ in what they carry and verify; and that this stage is next: the pool and the vault are. The sentence this stage would earn only after the ladder, every step passed, the composition measured and the minimum node inside its budget: "private money that confirms in seconds".
