## 3. One hash, and the conflicts resolved

H is Poseidon2 over Goldilocks as Miden VM 0.36.1 runs it natively (`hmerge`/`hperm`): width 12, rate 8, capacity 4, the S-box x^7, four initial and four terminal external rounds, twenty-two internal rounds, the Plonky3 Goldilocks permutation with the HorizenLabs round constants (miden-crypto 0.36.1, hash/algebraic_sponge/poseidon2). Every use is the two-to-one merge H(a, b): the state [a, b, 0, 0, 0, 0], one permutation, the first four elements out. A 32-byte value is a WORD: four canonical field elements, little-endian in order (miden's Word::as_bytes); the node refuses a commitment, a nullifier or an anchor that is not a word; the wallet reduces its derived secrets and its randomness into words (each limb modulo the prime: a bias of one part in 2^32 on one element, stated).

The definitions, the ADR's section 4.3 made exact in words of H (the vectors file names each):

- pk = H(sk_spend, nk);
- cm = H(H(value_word(v), pk), r), value_word(v) the four 32-bit limbs of the 128-bit value, limb 0 the least significant;
- nf = H(H(nk, cm), [position, 0, 0, 0]);
- a tree node H(left, right); the empty leaf the zero word; depth 32;
- the domain word [chain_id, kind, 0, 0], kind 1 a shield and 2 a spend;
- the shield statement hash H(H(domain, cm), value_word(v)); the spend statement hash H folded over [domain, anchor, nf1, nf2, cm1, cm2, value_word(v_pub), value_word(fee), address_word(public output)], the address as four 40-bit limbs.

The conflicts, resolved in the ADR's favour as the brief ordered: (a) the prover's cut-one statement had CM = H(H([v,0,0,0], hash(S)), R) and NF = H(S, [index,0,0,0]) with one owner secret S and a 64-bit v (the brief's own summary "H(owner_secret || leaf_index)" is that form); the ADR has two secrets (sk_spend, nk), pk = H(sk_spend, nk), and nf = H(nk, cm, position): the ADR's form stands, encoded as the nested merges above (the nullifier binds nk, the commitment and the position, so a viewing key holding nk computes nullifiers and a spending key alone does not spend without nk); (b) the pool's BLAKE3 Hasher and the wallet's BLAKE3 stand-in both become Poseidon2 (one implementation each, in Go and in plain Rust, no dependency on the prover's crates: the wallet workspace keeps its rust-version 1.80); (c) the wallet's 64-bit value becomes 128 bits (section 4); (d) the pool's record forms change (a shield carries a proof; a spend is exactly two in, two out) because the statement needs them (section 6).

The vectors (tools/money/vectors/money-stage1-vectors.json, written by money-vectors with miden-crypto itself): the permutation of [0..11] (miden-crypto's own known answer), a merge, a node, five value words, an address word, pk, cm and nf for fixed inputs, the empty roots at levels 1, 2 and 32, a shield statement hash, a spend statement hash, and the two program hashes. Three tests read the one file and pass: the Go TestSharedVectors (node/consensus/igneum/pool/vectors_test.go), the wallet's the_shared_vectors_hold (wallet/money/tests/shared_vectors.rs) and the prover's the_shared_vectors_hold (tools/money/stage1-prover/prover/tests/vectors.rs, which re-derives every line with miden-crypto and is the file's warrant). The Go and Rust permutations also carry the known answer in their own unit tests.

## 4. The value rule: 128 bits of wei in one word

Decided: a note's value is 128 bits of wei (the chain's unit, 10^18 a IGN), carried as four 32-bit limbs in one word, each limb range-checked in the circuit (u32assertw), the balance V1 + V2 = VO1 + VO2 + FEE + VPUB checked limb by limb with carries (u32overflowing_add3) and the final carries equal. Why this and not the other two: 64 bits cap a note at 18.4 IGN (cut three's finding) and the supply is 10^9 IGN = 10^27 wei, past 2^89; the ADR's "unit" alternative (a coarser unit than wei) would make the pool's value and the public side's value differ in unit, and the turnstile reconciles them to the wei (cut two's rule, kept: the lab reconciles 142,705,664,062,499,903,765 wei exactly); two 64-bit limbs do not fit Goldilocks elements (the prime is below 2^64), while four 32-bit limbs fill one word exactly and cost nothing extra in the hash. The node's maxValueBits was already 128; the wallet's note plaintext grows by eight bytes (version 2: 189 bytes; the output 1,325 bytes, the ciphertext 1,293; the ADR's "about 1,300" holds).

## 5. The security setting, and why 128 is not reachable in this release

Miden VM 0.36.1 ships ONE proof configuration (miden-air 0.36.1 src/config.rs, constants, no preset to pick): LOG_BLOWUP 3, 27 FRI queries, 17 bits of query proof-of-work, 12 bits of DEEP proof-of-work, 4 of folding, the quadratic extension of Goldilocks (a 128-bit challenge field). The verifier reports the proof's conjectured security as the minimum over seven terms (p3-security's budget). Unpatched, the cut-one shape of the new statement reads 96 bits, the query term binding. The patch of record (prepare-patched.sh copies the crate from the cargo registry into ./patched, never committed, and applies miden-air-queries.patch; the workspace's [patch.crates-io] builds the prover, the verifier and the node's library from the copy): NUM_QUERIES 27 to 31. The report at the patched setting, the verifier's own words for the lab's proofs:

| term | bits |
|---|---|
| lookup-challenge | 108 |
| constraint-composition | 119 |
| out-of-domain | 113 |
| deep-composition | 119 |
| fri-folding | 115 |
| fri-query | 108 (96 unpatched) |
| commitment-collision | 127 |

So the conjectured level is 108 bits, bound by the lookup-challenge term, a function of the 128-bit challenge field and the lookup shape alone: no count of queries or proof-of-work bits in this release moves it, and 128 needs the cubic extension (a 192-bit challenge field), which 0.36.1 fixes as the quadratic one in its types, not in a constant. The patch is therefore the most this release can state, recorded as such; the node's MinSecurityBits is 108 and the verifier refuses a proof below it (a proof made under the unpatched setting is refused as "below the minimum"). The cost of the patch: the spend proof 67,660 bytes at 96 bits to 75,660 at 108 (the bench, 96 threads), proving 111.6 ms to 106.0 ms on 96 threads (within the noise), verification 1.04 ms to 1.00 ms. The 128-bit figure the ADR asks for is an ESTIMATE from here: the cubic extension's field elements are half again as wide, so 1.3x to 1.5x the proof (98 to 113 KB, inside the 140 KB threshold) and the same order of proving time; not measured.

## 8. The two additions of 21:0x UK, and what of each landed

**Proof pruning as a rule.** The rule as stated: a proof is verified once at import and then dropped; a node retains only the commitments, the nullifiers, the ciphertexts and the records' public fields; a node that serves history may keep proofs behind a flag. Landed in this cut: the retained set defined and measured (section 7: 2,835 bytes a spend, 1,328 a shield), and served as such: igneum_poolBlock gives a wallet exactly that set for a block (the admitted records read against the block's and the parent's state, so a refused record never appears), which is what the wallets scan in the lab; the pool's consensus state (the tree, the nullifier set, the turnstile) holds no proof bytes already. NOT landed: the drop itself. In this node's form a record's proof sits in the transaction's data inside the block body, and the body is covered by the header's transaction root; dropping proof bytes from stored bodies means a node that no longer serves whole bodies, and a syncing peer that takes pruned history from an archive reader (behind a flag) and trusts the archive's verification or re-verifies from one: that is the pruned-sync path of ADR 4.6 row 5, a sync-protocol design, named here as the next node cut after the vault's statement, not done quietly inside this one. The figures above are the rule's, measured; the mechanism is not.

**The seed birthday.** Landed: the wallet file carries the seed's creation height ("birthday"); keygen takes --birthday (the chain's height at creation; the lab passes the head), restore takes --birthday and says in words when it is absent ("this wallet scans from genesis"), backup prints the height after the words, and scan starts at the birthday (or --from-height), skipping the blocks below it by their positions (the stream from igneum_poolBlock carries each block's first position, so the skipped outputs still count); a block below the birthday without a position is refused rather than guessed. The restore time at 30,000 transfers a day (60,000 outputs a day, 21.9 million a year, 28.3 GB of ciphertexts a year), measured on one EPYC core at 32.06 us a ciphertext (the bench-scan of cut three, re-run on this hash): a wallet one year old restores in 11.7 minutes of trial decryption with the birthday, whatever the chain's age; without the birthday it scans the whole chain: 11.7 minutes a chain-year (a two-year chain 23 minutes, three years 35), plus the download of 28.3 GB a chain-year either way. A phone at a factor of 3 to 10 (ESTIMATE): 35 minutes to two hours a year with the birthday. The lab's restore rows (section 6) ran both ways to the same balance.

**The shield's value binding (found in this cut, closed).** Cut two's shield carried a commitment the node could not check against the value: a shield of one wei could have carried a commitment claiming 10^30, worthless outside the pool (the turnstile bounds what leaves) but able to drain other holders' deposits from inside it. Now a shield carries a proof of money-shield-v1 (the commitment's hidden value equals the public value, pk and r hidden; 56 KB, 13 ms) and the lab's refusal row shows the mismatch refused. For the register: a cut-two finding, cured here.

## 9. Caveats

- The 128-bit row of ADR 4.6 is not met and cannot be in Miden VM 0.36.1 (section 5); 108 conjectured bits is the measured ceiling, the bound conjectured, not proven.
- No N100 figure: the machine is not enrolled anywhere reachable; the lab box A figures are an EPYC core's; the ADR's factor of 2 to 10 is applied, not measured.
- No phone figure; no delegation built.
- The pruned-sync path and the body drop are not built (section 8); the growth figures are extrapolations from measured record sizes, not a day's run.
- F-761 stays open: the carrier is a public transaction from the faucet key paying gas, the statement's fee is burned whole from the pool; the signerless type and the fee split are the design item.
- The statement binds the record's public fields and the chain, not the ciphertexts: a relay that swapped an output's ciphertext would leave the commitment admitted and the note undeliverable (no value moves; the sender's outgoing record still opens it). Binding B(ciphertexts) into the statement hash is a one-word change named for the vault cut.
- A dummy input (value zero) skips the membership check by the statement's rule (Sapling's), and its nullifier is published and marked like any other; a dummy output is a zero-value note to a key nobody holds with random ciphertext bytes, one output shape for every transfer.
- igneum_poolPath reads every leaf (the lab's form, refused past 2^22 leaves); a node-side tree index is the production form.
- The sealer runs Finalize twice a block (its assembly and its import): a spend costs it 6 ms of verification a block, not 3.
- The wallet reduces derived secrets and randomness into words with a 2^-32 bias on one element in 2^32 draws.
