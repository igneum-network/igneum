## 2. What was built (the construction as cut, and the choices the design left open)

The design's section 2 as the node now runs it under the landing check, in the order of the design's "changed, one at a time":

1. **The block DAG and the rate.** A header carries its selected parent in ParentHash as every block does and its MERGED parents in a new tail field, Parents (core/types/block.go: 32-byte hashes concatenated, RLP-optional so an empty field encodes as absent and the headers of the chains of record are byte for byte what they were; inside the seal hash from the review's fourth reading, F-803: as the lab ran, the field was OUTSIDE sealFields, so one search could have yielded many valid blocks differing in their mergesets; cured on the landing with TestDAGParentsInsideTheSealHash, the lab's blocks sealed under the earlier preimage and quarantined). The engine's rule (consensus/igneum/dagform.go verifyDAGHeader): at most 16 parents (Kaspa's bound), distinct, known, an antichain (no parent in another's past: the simpler form, taken where the design did not say), the selected parent the one of greatest blue work (ties to the greater hash), the mergeset above the merge-depth root. The merged rate is one block every 4 seconds by three lanes each at a 12-second target under its own controller.
2. **The lanes as parallel streams.** The three coordinates of the vector form on the null algorithm (the lab's stand-in for the three kernels: a keccak search, two CPU threads a sealer, two sealers a lane across the two boxes), each sealer naming every other tip it holds as a merged parent (TemplateParents: the tips by blue work, at most 15). The weight is FORM A, the additive measure: a block's own work is D(b) x 2^32 / DRef(lane of b), the blue work the selected parent's plus the mergeset blues' (dagWork, dag.Ghostdag). Form B is step 1's and was not cut.
3. **The ordering.** consensus/igneum/dag is GHOSTDAG as rusty-kaspa states it (protocol.rs, mergeset.rs, ordering.rs, read on the day): the mergeset walked in ascending (blue work, hash) order, a candidate blue when its anticone within the new block's blue set holds at most k blocks and no blue's blue anticone would exceed k by its addition (the blues-anticone-sizes map carried as Kaspa carries it), the blue score the selected parent's plus the mergeset blues, the ordering the selected chain with each block's mergeset in (blue work, hash) order after its selected parent. k = 4, computed by Kaspa's own table (dag.K, calculate_ghostdag_k) at 2 D lambda = 1 (D = 2 s the design's target, lambda one block every 4 s) and delta 0.01; the same function gives Kaspa's 124 at its 10 blocks a second (TestKMatchesKaspa). Reproduced against the six rusty-kaspa test DAGs (testing/integration/testdata/dags, dag0 the PHANTOM paper's figure at k = 4): every block's selected parent, mergeset blues in colouring order, mergeset reds and blue score exactly as the implementation of record asserts them (TestGhostdagAgainstKaspaVectors), the virtual block's reds equal where the files state them. The tie among equal blue work is the HASH (Kaspa's form): the design keeps the sealer's registered lot, which is the sortition rule's and the lab chain runs none; the simpler form, stated here. Reachability is a depth-pruned walk back over parents (dag.IsAncestor), not Kaspa's interval tree: the lab form, its cost in section 4.
4. **The fork choice** is the selected chain of the virtual block: the head is the tip of greatest blue work, ties to the greater hash, decided before the vector form's rules (vector.go PreferByWeight, dagPreferByWeight). First-seen at equal height and the withholding length condition have no object under the landing check, as the design says.
5. **The state.** A block's state is its selected parent's after the ordered mergeset's transactions and then its own (core/dag_mergeset.go ApplyMergeset, ONE function run by the state processor on import and by the miner at assembly): each merged block's transactions in their order, each against the carrying block's base fee (its own market, the burn that block's) with the tip to the merging block's coinbase; a transaction failing its pre-check at its ordered position (a nonce already used, a balance gone, a fee cap under the base fee) is NOT accepted, its state untouched, its tip unpaid, the block valid; the merging block's own transactions must execute (its header's receipts root and gas are its own). The acceptance record (which transactions a block accepted and which it refused, with the reason) is kept by the node and served by igneum_dagTx; it is not in the header (the receipts root covers the block's own transactions), a reading the design's EVM row leaves to the ladder and this step states as a choice.
6. **Finality's three lines.** CONFIRMED at a blue-score depth above the accepting block (section 1's clock); SETTLED at the merge-depth bound, cut as a validity rule (DAGMergeDepth 150: a block whose mergeset holds a block without the merge-depth root in its past is refused, the root the selected-chain block 150 blue blocks back); PRUNABLE (the finality depth, pruning) not cut.
7. **Propagation.** The fetcher asks the pushing peer for a block's missing selected and merged parents by hash (a number-less announcement, eth/fetcher/block_fetcher.go announceHash; the block waits aside for the first of them, F-203's form), and the handler asks any peer every two seconds for the merged parents a batch import found unknown (the gap filler); the downloader's sync by number is untouched.
8. **The readers.** igneum_dagBlock (a block's parents, selected parent, mergeset blues and reds, blue score, blue work, on the chain or not, blue or red in the head's view, the arrival clock, the acceptance record), igneum_dagHead (the gate, k, the merge depth, the head, the tips), igneum_dagTx (a transaction's carrying blocks and its accepting chain block with its order against the head's), igneum_dagChain (the selected chain with each block's ordered mergeset and an ordering hash per order), igneum_dagReorgs.

## 5. The rows run and their outcomes

Each row by its command on the lab (tools/money/lab/ordering-step1.sh), its reading from the driver's record (result.json, the 30-second head samples and the clocks) and the nodes' logs.

- **The pre-peering forks (the first merge, unplanned and kept).** The eight nodes sealed for 40 seconds before the mesh was peered (the enode form was wrong on the first try: admin.nodeInfo.id is not the enode's key), so each sealer held a chain of its own from the genesis. At the peering the ordering merged them: blocks 8, 9, 10 and 12 merged the solo chains with mergesets of 11, 4, 2 and 3 (one blue each, the selected parent; 10, 3, 1 and 2 red), every node agreeing on the colouring (the ordering hashes equal from order 0). The reds are the k-cluster rule: a run of seven parallel blocks exceeds k = 4, and the paper's prediction (a late-released private branch is red) read on the chain's first page.
- **The double spend across parallel blocks.** Nineteen pairs: one nonce, two recipients, the two transactions sent at the same instant to two different sealers (one on each box in most pairs). Every pair was decided with exactly one accepted and the other never in any block: at 0.6 ms the first transaction reached the second sealer's pool by gossip before the second arrived, and that pool refused the second as a same-price replacement of a known nonce, so no pair reached two parallel blocks. The ordering's rule for the case the design states (two blocks in each other's anticone carrying the two) was exercised on the chain by the partition rows alone, where lab box A's nine red blocks' two transactions were re-applied at 258's ordered position and accepted (no conflict in them), and is proven by the unit test. The rule itself is the unit test's: TestDAGConflictAcrossParallelBlocks builds two children of the genesis each carrying one of two transactions of one nonce and a block that merges the loser; the loser is not accepted ("nonce too low" at its ordered position), its recipient holds nothing, the sender's nonce is one, the loser's tip is unpaid, the two coinbases hold Reward(1) and Reward(2) and the merging block's nothing.
- **The partition of 60 seconds.** The row of record (08:22:55Z to 08:23:56Z; the first attempt at 08:19:44Z never partitioned, section 6 item 2): every cross-box enode removed from every node once a second for a minute. The boxes diverged at once (every lab box A node on cece1f…, every lab box B node on b5b1c8… at the minute's end, three peers each). The rejoin at 08:23:57Z: the downloader took lab box B's branch on lab box A (a reorganisation of depth 9 at 246, "drop=9 add=9" in R2's log at 08:23:58.969), block 258 (lane 0, lab box B) merged lab box A's abandoned branch of nine as its mergeset, one blue and nine red, and every node was on one head by 08:24:03Z, six seconds after the rejoin. The second row (09:24:13Z to 09:25:17Z) ran the same way with the sides swapped (lab box A's branch won; no reorganisation on R2, one on lab box B). Across the eight nodes the rows made the reorganisations of depth 5, 8 and 9 in section 4 and the 34 red blocks; the transactions in the losing branches were re-applied through the mergeset. The merged parents were asked for by hash through the fetcher ("Requesting a DAG parent by hash" did not fire on R2: the downloader's batch sync brought the branch with its parents in order, and the merging block's merged parent was then already known), so the gap filler was not needed on this topology.
- **The lane stopped for ten minutes.** Lane 2's two sealers (S2 on lab box A, T2 on lab box B) stopped by miner_stop at 08:25:09Z and restarted at 08:35:19Z. For the ten minutes the merged rate was 4.96 s a block (121 blocks: the two running lanes at their 12-second targets, 6 s merged, with the lane-2 sealers' last blocks in the window), the ordering unchanged (no merge beside the selected parent, no red, the tips one on every node), the lane's difficulty falling under its controller (the absolute form: the lane's blue count against the clock). On the restart the console's miner.start() without a thread count set 96 threads on each (the node's miner_start default is every core), so lane 2 came back at 48 times its rate and with a halved difficulty: it sealed 60 of the next 100 chain blocks, the merged rate 1.85 s a block over the ten minutes (325 blocks), until its controller had lifted its difficulty to 887,744,140 (17.75 times its reference, against 0.74 and 0.90 for lanes 1 and 0 at 10:35Z) and the lanes were back at one block each every 12 s (the last 200 chain blocks before 10:03Z: 60 / 71 / 69, 4.07 s a block). The ordering took no notice either way; what changed is the WEIGHT: under form A as cut (a block's work D / DRef with the registered DRef of 50,000,000) a lane-2 block then weighed 17.75 against 0.74 to 0.90, about twenty times a block of the other lanes, so lane 2 held about 87 percent of every block's added blue work from then on. ## 6. What broke and what was learned

1. **Form A as the design writes it gives a chip the weight of its hash rate; and a stopped lane comes back with a burst.** Lane 2's two sealers were stopped for ten minutes (08:25:09Z to 08:35:19Z). The merged rate fell to the other two lanes' (4.96 s a block over the ten minutes against 3.7 to 4.0 before), the ordering did not notice (no merge, no red: a lane is not a party to the ordering, only its blocks are), and the lane's controller (the absolute form of the anchored ASERT: the lane's blue count against the clock since the genesis) halved its difficulty over the ten minutes. The restart was miner.start() at the console, whose default is EVERY core ("Updated mining threads threads=96" in both logs), so lane 2 came back at 48 times its rate with a halved difficulty and sealed 60 of the next 100 chain blocks at 1.85 s a block merged, the controller lifting its difficulty to 17.75 times its reference against 0.74 to 0.90 for the other lanes before the lanes were back at one block each every 12 s. Two readings. (i) The controller's debt: the absolute form makes a returning lane repay its missing count at double rate until the clock agrees; the design's lane-difficulty row names a SAMPLED WINDOW (Kaspa's 2,641 s sampled every 4 s), which forgets the debt; this cut kept today's controller, the simpler form, and the catch-up is its price, to be cut before the chip rows. (ii) THE WEIGHT: form A's measure is D(b) / DRef with the lane's REGISTERED reference (the design's words: "each lane's block counted as one target block at its reference"), and a lane whose difficulty the controller has lifted twentyfold weighs twenty times a block of the others, so a party with a chip on one lane holds the weight of its hash rate (here about 87 percent of every block's added blue work), not "a third of the weight at most" as the design's section 2.2 item 2 reasons from the controller holding the lane's block RATE. The rate is held; the weight is not, because the reference does not follow the difficulty. The cut before step 1's chip rows: normalise a block's work by the lane's difficulty IN FORCE at its height (every blue block then counts one, whatever its lane's hash rate, and the design's third holds by construction), or carry form B. Nothing on 7411 went wrong from it (no adversary, one head everywhere), which is exactly why it must be read as a finding and not as a pass.
2. **A single admin_removePeer does not partition two boxes.** The first partition (08:19:44Z) removed the other box's four enodes from every local node once, on both boxes, and the boxes never diverged: a surviving link re-dialled from the side whose removal had not yet run, and every block crossed it (the vault lab's lesson, "admin_removePeer leaks through a third node's static entry", read again). The row of record (08:22:55Z to 08:23:56Z) removes every cross enode every second for the minute; the heads diverged within seconds (section 5).
3. **A nine-block branch is red, and the partition's transactions survive through the mergeset.** At the rejoin the downloader took lab box B's heavier branch (the reorganisation of depth 9 at 246), and lab box A's branch of nine blocks became the mergeset of block 258: one blue (the selected parent) and nine red, the k-cluster rule at k = 4 exactly as the paper predicts for a parallel branch longer than k; their two transactions were re-applied at 258's ordered position and accepted. The design's withholding reading ("a withheld branch released late is red and adds nothing") read on the chain without an attacker.
4. **The pre-peering forks.** The nodes sealed for 40 seconds before the mesh was peered (the enode form was wrong on the first try: admin.nodeInfo.id is the 64-hex node id, the enode needs the 128-hex key, so the lab script now reads admin.nodeInfo.enode and rewrites its address to the mesh); the ordering merged the eight solo chains at blocks 8, 9, 10 and 12 (mergesets of 11, 4, 2 and 3; 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 blue, 10 + 3 + 1 + 2 red) and every node agreed. An unplanned merge row, kept as the first evidence that the colouring agrees across nodes on a non-trivial DAG.
5. **At 0.6 ms the DAG is a chain.** Outside the rows the tips were one on every node at every sample and no block was merged beside its selected parent: 2 D lambda on this mesh is three orders of magnitude under the design's 1, so the parallel-block path was exercised by the rows and the unit tests, not by the steady state (section 7's first caveat). The geographic row is the one that measures the ordering's reason to exist.
6. **The test's roots.** GenerateChain's blocks must be made under the same gated engine the chain runs, or their state roots carry the chain form's subsidy and the import refuses them ("invalid merkle root", the double-spend test's first run); and a refused transaction's tip is not paid, which the test's first expectation got wrong and the design's section 4 states ("no fee is paid for it because it never executed").
7. **debug_metrics is not served by this node**, so the bandwidth row reads the kernel's socket counters on the p2p ports (cumulative per connection, reset by the partition row; an undercount of what crossed before 08:24Z).
8. **The acceptance record is recomputed, not committed** (section 7). The driver's clock reads it through igneum_dagTx on one reader; a transaction carried by a red block and refused at the merge shows as "refused" there until a later block accepts it, which none did for the conflicting pairs' losers (section 5).


## 7. Caveats, plainly

- **No pool yet.** The ordering ran with plain transfers; the pool's nullifier race, the anchor window and the turnstile under parallel blocks (the design's section 4) are step 4's and were not measured. "Confirms in seconds" for the pool is a property of transfers of settled notes, as the design states, and nothing here speaks to it.
- **The lab's latency against a real network's.** The two boxes sit in one hall on a mesh with 0.6 ms round trip; the measured D per lane (section 4) is that of a LAN, and at it 2 D lambda is about 0.0003, so the DAG is a chain almost everywhere (the tips were one on every node at every 30-second sample outside the rows) and the k of 4, set from the design's D of 2 s, was exercised only by the pre-peering forks and the partition row. The confirmation figures are therefore the figures of an unloaded, undelayed network: a lower bound on what a continental network would read, not the measurement of record for the target. The geographic row (DEVNET.md G2's continental D) is still unread.
- **The null algorithm.** The lanes were three CPU keccak searches, not the three kernels: the block verification cost (section 4) carries no proof-of-work check of the kernels' kind, no shares and no payout lists, so G1's 250 ms is read here on the ordering's own cost and the EVM's alone.
- **Two sealers a lane, on CPU, at equal power.** No difficulty asymmetry between lanes beyond the controllers' noise; the chip rows are not run.
- **The reachability walk.** dag.IsAncestor walks parents back pruned by depth, the lab form; Kaspa keeps an interval tree. At a thin DAG the walk is microseconds (section 4); at k = 124 it would not be.
- **The acceptance record is node-local.** The receipts root covers a block's own transactions; which merged transactions a block accepted is recomputed by every node from the same state transition (it must be, or the state root would differ) but is not committed in the header. A reader that trusts a header alone cannot read it; step 7's RPC form decides whether it should be committed.
- **Settlement is the merge-depth bound, not finality.** A block 150 blue blocks deep cannot be reordered by MERGING on any node that follows the rule; a heavier chain that forks below it and never merges it is the classic majority attack, outside the bound as it is outside Kaspa's. No pruning point was cut.
- **The tie is the hash.** The design keeps the sealer's lot at equal blue work; this cut takes Kaspa's hash tie on a chain without the sortition rule. The lot is step 1's or step 3's to restore.
- **One run.** 2,044 blocks of one lab on one day; the reordering distribution and the red fraction are that run's.
