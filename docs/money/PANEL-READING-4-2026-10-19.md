## 1. lead/money-vault-findings ed3136377 (F-799, F-800 and its re-cut ef8314ed7, F-801)

**Ruling: PASS WITH RE-CUTS.** The three cures are what the third reading asked for, each with a test that fails on main and passes after (read in the diffs: TestVaultRevocationAtTheAnnouncement, the prover's refusals on announce and rec-cancel, TestVaultHeadRaceOrdered); the five rows and the three new rows hold to the wei at both scales. Two wording re-cuts applied by the review on the page (F-802, F-804); no code fault.

The F-800 re-cut, read hardest:

- **The bit cannot be forged or replayed.** The co-signature bit is written by money-vault-announce-v1 into head_out's IDX word (element 3, mem 195 from mem 420) inside the head commitment that is the statement's public CM_HEAD_OUT; the circuit admits the bit only as 0 or 1 (b x b = b) and, at 1, only with SK_E and NK_E opening the policy's pk_e (mem 100). money-vault-rec-cancel-v1 reads it from head_in opened under the anchor and the nullifier (open_head, mem 155), so a cancellation cannot claim a bit the announcement did not prove. Every head-consuming statement consumes the head's nullifier, so an announcement is not replayable; withdraw, policy-complete and rec-cancel rewrite the IDX word with element 3 zero (withdraw and policy-complete `push.192 exec.zero_word` then element 0 alone; rec-cancel the same), and withdraw is refused while a recovery is pending, so the bit is 1 exactly while a co-signed recovery is pending. The branch in rec-cancel (pk_c differs AND bit) is read from the announced policy whose hash must equal the head's rec_hash (mem 332 against mem 160), so the cancellation key's holder cannot present a kept-pk_c policy to take the easier branch.
- **Both of the owner's keys stolen.** The page names it (section 7): with the everyday key and the cancellation key both compromised the authority's rotation cannot be co-signed and the stolen cancellation key cancels it; outside the spec's one-key-at-a-time table. The panel accepts the limit as stated: the everyday key is the key the spec assumes stolen, and the cancellation key's compromise is run 5's row, where the owner holds the everyday key. A spec re-cut names the pair as outside the model, with the remedy the t-of-n authority of the first page's section 10 (a quorum the owner's two keys do not control).
- **F-802 (design, the co-signature's witness).** The co-signed announcement is one witness carrying sk_a and sk_e together (the advice order in vault-announce.masm; prepare_announce writes both secrets into one witness file). A recovery authority that is a separate party, which is the spec's form (user-chosen; the t-of-n guardians of S_rec_announce), cannot co-sign without the owner's everyday secret, and the owner cannot co-sign the authority's announcement without sk_a. The lab's run 5 holds because one prover held both; the rule is sound where the owner is its own authority. Not a hole in the chain's rule (a co-signed rotation is still the authority's alone to cancel) but a hole in who can produce one. The next cut: the owner's consent as its own statement (the everyday key consumes the head and sets the bit with the announced policy's hash, before the authority's announcement, which then reads it), or two proofs in one record. Also: the co-signer reads the new policy it is given; the wallet should print the changed fields (pk_c above all) before it co-signs, which is the "tricked into co-signing" case's only defence inside the wallet. Applied as a caveat on the page's section 7.
- **The revocation binds from the block after the announcement.** Confirmed in AdmitVault: a completion is checked against the revoked root as it stood at the parent (F-778), and the completion reveals no policy hash, so the node cannot refuse it by the announcing block's own insertion; F-801's first pass applies the announcement before the completion in the same block but the completion's proof is against the parent's root and is admitted. The window is one block and the page says so. The panel adds no rule: under the floors the thief's matured note is one the thief had not completed, and the announcement reaches it one block later.
- **The scaled delays as evidence.** Rows A, B and C and runs 4 and 5 are ORDER rows (which statement is admitted or refused, and by which key), not timing rows, so their meaning does not depend on the scale; run 4 and the three rows also completed at full scale before the stop. No row of this page depends on the full scale. Two cautions, both stated on the page: run 3 at the scaled delays is not a row (the driver's 60-block offset against the 8-block margin), and the scaled build re-pins create and announce (the floor constants inside check_floors), so the scaled lab's verifier is the committed logic under other constants, not the committed binary; the committed pins (announce 0x807a179fa18e0bc9, rec-cancel 0xe815e83f30a33e41) are the full-scale run's and the miden package's New checks them at every start. The lab clock rule stands.
- **F-804 (wording, the revealed hash's privacy).** The announcement now reveals the old policy hash, which the spec kept private until S_rec_complete, so the three records of one recovery are linkable by the word and a holder of the vault's deposit address (any depositor) learns of a recovery from the announcing block rather than the completion. The price of F-799; the page's section 7 now says it; the spec's re-cut carries it.
- **F-799 and F-801, read.** revokedRemove rebuilds the indexed tree from the kept leaves (the sentinel at key 0 never removed; the next pointers recomputed; the last slot cleared; the count and root rewritten), listed in tools/ci/finalize-writes.txt with four writes; the policy hash is per vault (the policy's nk_field is H(nk_v) with nk_v from the seed and the vault id, and the nonce is a random word), so an announcement on one vault revokes nothing of another. pool.Ordered is the only order Finalize and Extract walk (settlePool and Extract both call it; the pruner at eth/pool_prune.go walks transactions in place and reads no position); the first pass is announce, rec-cancel, cancel; shields and spends are second-pass records, so their positions follow a block's first-pass records on every node alike. The transaction pool and the block's transaction order are unchanged; vault statements carry no value, so the EVM's order is of no consequence.
- Noted for the spec's re-cut (the page's section 8): rule 7 as "revoked at the announcement, restored by the cancellation"; S_rec_cancel's authority by the bit; the block order; the reclaim statement for the revoked notes of a completed recovery.
